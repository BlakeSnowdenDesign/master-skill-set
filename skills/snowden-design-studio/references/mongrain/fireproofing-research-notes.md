## What can inform generic explanations

| Topic | Knowledge supported by reviewed sources | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Structural steel protection | Passive fire protection slows heat transfer to steel; strength and stiffness decline as steel heats. | Do not imply steel must melt before it loses load capacity or assign one universal collapse temperature. |
| Intumescent coatings / IFRM | Heat causes a specified coating to expand into insulating char; a paint-like finish can preserve the visible steel profile before exposure. | Char expansion, dry-film thickness, primer/topcoat compatibility and rating are product/design dependent. |
| Spray-applied fire-resistive material / SFRM | Spray-applied insulating protection slows steel heating; composition and finish vary by system. | Do not depict all traditional fireproofing as one universal cement formulation or equate board encasement with sprayed protection. |
| Boards and encasement | Board systems are another family of structural fire protection. | Their joints, fixings, thickness and member details must follow a supported design. They are not interchangeable with sprays. |
| Fire-resistance-rated assemblies | Ratings depend on complete assemblies and published construction/installation criteria. | A product label or generic layer diagram is not proof that a wall, floor, roof, beam or column achieves a rating. |
| Perimeter fire containment | The floor-slab-to-curtain-wall joint is protected by a coordinated tested system. | Mineral wool alone is not a sufficient generic specification. The design may require safing, coating/sealant, attachment, spandrel insulation, mullion protection and reinforcement. |
| Exterior wall fire propagation | Combustible exterior-wall systems have distinct fire-spread evaluation issues. | Do not substitute an exterior-wall propagation test for an hourly structural/assembly rating. |
| Gypsum assemblies | Fire-resistance testing evaluates configured assemblies under prescribed exposure. | Laboratory classification does not predict the exact outcome of every real building fire. |

### Canadian jurisdiction

NRC lists NBC 2025 and NFC 2025 as national model-code publications. A publication date is not proof of adoption in a province or territory. Establish the project's jurisdiction, adopted edition, amendments, transition provisions and authority requirements before describing compliance. Do not label NBC 2025 as the governing Québec code by default.

Several linked resources are US-facing. UL 263/ASTM E119, ASTM E2307 and NFPA 285 explain different test contexts; do not treat their names, results or US code references as automatically accepted Canadian compliance. Confirm the applicable Canadian test designation, edition and acceptance for the actual project.

## Implications for the current five-service explorer

These are review findings, not changes already made to the live embed.

| Existing wording/graphic | Required treatment before claiming verification |
| --- | --- |
| “Up to 3 h fire resistance” | Attach a relevant approved/tested design and its member/material conditions, or replace with a nonnumeric explanation of project-specific ratings. |
| “Largest project in Canada” | Require approved company/project evidence identifying category, date and comparison basis. Manufacturer resources cannot verify it. |
| “Steel below failure temp.” | Prefer “Slows steel heating.” A generic animation cannot demonstrate the steel temperature or structural capacity. |
| Dry film → expanded char illustration | Keep as a qualitative mechanism, not a measured expansion ratio or performance simulation. Label illustrative/NTS where appropriate. |
| Traditional fireproofing drawing | Make clear whether the depiction is spray-applied protection or board encasement; do not suggest they are the same installation. |
| Thermal barrier explanation | Obtain substrate/foam/product and applicable assembly/test evidence before adding ratings, universal thicknesses or installation prescriptions. |
| Firewalls & partitions | Explain the intended category without suggesting every partition is a code-defined fire wall. Require assembly evidence for ratings. |
| Decontamination | Keep separate from the fireproofing mechanism. Use qualified survey and jurisdiction-specific hazardous-material sources before expanding technical copy. |

Safe generic copy examples:

> Intumescent coatings expand into an insulating char when exposed to heat, slowing heat transfer to structural steel. The required coating system and thickness depend on the approved design and project conditions.

> Spray-applied fire-resistive materials form an insulating layer around structural members. Material selection, thickness and installation follow the applicable tested design.

> Perimeter fire containment protects the joint between a rated floor and a curtain wall through a coordinated tested system—not simply by filling the gap with insulation.

## Repeatable evidence workflow

1. Identify jurisdiction, system family, existing approved copy and the exact claim being made.
2. Use this register to find the right authority/manufacturer; reopen current originals before a new technical assertion.
3. Record title, URL, date reviewed, relevant page/section, jurisdiction and what was actually inspected.
4. For numerical ratings, thicknesses, spacing, temperatures or installation requirements, obtain the actual supported design and conditions. Do not interpolate a specification from a conceptual graphic.
5. Keep three evidence classes distinct: technical mechanism, product/assembly evidence and Mongrain project/company evidence.
6. Use generic system labels in public Mongrain diagrams; keep manufacturer names in source citations unless branding is expressly requested.
7. Check copy and drawings against the same evidence. Mark conceptual diagrams as illustrative; animation is not a fire test.
8. List unresolved claims explicitly. Update the preview/export only when that is the requested scope; updating these notes or GitHub does not update existing Wix HTML.
